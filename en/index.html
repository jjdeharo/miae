A guide to getting started

MIAE helps students and teachers describe generative AI use, agree how it may be used in a task and explain the process they followed.

  1. Define the task

    Identify who performs it and which product or component you are classifying: a report, its illustration or the analysis of a text.

  2. Consider the whole process

    Describe what AI contributed and what the person did with it. The first prompt, the amount of text and the number of messages do not determine the level.

  3. Explain the classification

    Justify the level through the division of work. If phases or components differ, declare their respective levels rather than automatically choosing the highest.

Levels describe how work is shared. A higher level does not mean better quality or more learning, and there is no need to go through the earlier levels.

One ecosystem report, six processes

In every case the product is a report. What changes is how it is developed. Select a level to read its full definition.

0The person creates

The person observes the ecosystem, consults sources and writes the report without generative AI at any stage.

1AI reformulates

The person provides fully developed observations, explanations and conclusions. AI improves their expression without adding content or changing the relationships between ideas.

2AI plans

AI suggests questions and an outline to guide the work. The person researches and independently develops all the explanations and the final wording.

3The person builds

AI writes an initial report. The person reconstructs its content: revises the ecological relationships, incorporates their own data and develops new explanations and conclusions of their own.

4Co-creation

The person and AI develop the report through dialogue. The person contributes observations, challenges an explanation and proposes another; both contributions shape the content as it is developed.

5The person supervises

The person provides the topic, data and requirements. AI develops the entire report; the person checks its accuracy and approves it without reconstructing or co-creating its content.

Frequently asked questions

Is writing with AI always level 5?

No. If the person provides fully developed content and AI only expresses or faithfully reformulates it, it is level 1. Giving only a topic is not the same as providing that development.

What distinguishes level 2 from level 3?

At level 2, AI prepares ideas or structure and the person develops the content. At level 3, there is already a draft containing AI-developed content which the person substantially reconstructs. If the requested product is the outline itself, its creation must be classified as a product.

Do many requests for changes imply level 4?

No. There must be shared content development with human contributions and judgment. Requests for stylistic changes or acceptance of suggestions are not enough. At level 5, the person supervises the product; at level 3, they mainly reconstruct it themselves.

What if the text and images have different levels?

Declare them separately and explain their relationship to the whole. For example: text without AI and a level 5 illustration. The highest level is not automatically assigned to the entire project.

Does the level determine what is permitted or what grade the work deserves?

Not on its own. The teacher must specify permitted uses according to the task’s objective. The level does not measure learning, quality or effort.

Is analysing an AI-generated text level 5?

Generating the text may be level 5. If the teacher supplies it and students analyse it without AI, the analysis may be level 0. If a student commissions the text and then analyses it, both parts should be described.

Try four cases

Choose a classification and check the reasoning. You are classifying the described process, not the quality of its result.

1A student gives AI her complete arguments and conclusions. AI writes them clearly without adding ideas. Which level describes this help?

View explanation

Level 1: AI reformulates human-developed content. The amount of text it writes does not make this level 5.

2A teacher asks AI for a complete report, checks the data and accepts the result with minor stylistic edits. Which level describes how the report was developed?

View explanation

Level 5: autonomous generation and supervision. Minor edits are neither substantial reconstruction at level 3 nor co-creation at level 4.

3AI proposes an ecological explanation; a student spots a contradiction, contributes her data and an alternative hypothesis, and both parties develop a new explanation through dialogue. Which level applies?

View explanation

Level 4: the person and AI contribute to content development. Shared development is decisive, not the number of messages.

4AI suggests an outline. A student writes all the content without AI. AI then corrects the expression without adding ideas. How is the process best described?

View explanation

Mixed process: level 2 for planning and level 1 for reformulating human-developed content. Both phases should be declared.

What version 2.1 clarifies

It retains the six levels of revised version 2 and specifies how to apply them:

  • Reformulation can start from fully developed notes and arguments, even if they have not been written as a final text.
  • Classification considers the whole process and may change if reconstruction or co-creation follows.
  • Mixed processes are described by phase or component, without automatically taking the highest level.
  • The boundaries between planning, reconstruction, co-creation and supervision are made more explicit.
